Common Questions

How the monthly reconciliation works

Straight answers — this is a new process, so ask if anything below doesn't match what you're seeing.

Why are we doing this now?

We used to rely on contractors flagging closed jobs themselves, and that let some go untracked and unbilled on both sides. This form makes it a fixed monthly step instead of something that only happens if someone remembers to bring it up.

What counts as a "closed job" for this form?

Any job that came from a lead we sent you and that you were paid for — quoted, booked, and completed (or a deposit taken on a booked job), regardless of when the lead itself came in. If a lead from July closes in August, report it in August's confirmation.

How and when do you invoice?

After you submit the form, we total the value you report and invoice 10% of it within a few business days. Payment terms are net 15 from the invoice date — by e-transfer or however we've already been settling up.

What if I miss a month or forget to submit?

Give us a shout and we'll work it out — life happens. But if a couple of months go by with no confirmation and no response, we'll reach out directly before assuming nothing closed, since that also means we can't tell if the leads we're sending are actually converting for you.

What if I under-report or a number's off?

Tell us and we'll fix it — this isn't meant to be adversarial. On larger jobs we may ask for a quick confirmation (an invoice screenshot is fine) if a number looks out of line with the leads we tracked on our end.

What happens if an invoice goes unpaid?

We don't lock anyone into a contract, so there's no penalty clause to enforce — but we also don't keep sending exclusive leads to an account that isn't paying for the ones already sent. Past due 15 days, we'll follow up directly. If it goes past 30 days with no payment and no word back, we pause new leads to that account until it's settled, and the trade/territory may be reopened to another contractor. We'd always rather sort it out with a phone call first.
